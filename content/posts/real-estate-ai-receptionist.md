In real estate, the agent who replies first wins. That's not a slogan — it's the math. Industry research from MIT and Harvard Business Review has shown for over a decade that **replying within 5 minutes makes you 21x more likely to qualify the lead** than replying after 30 minutes.

Yet most real estate agents reply to inquiries in 2-12 hours. They're showing other properties, in closings, at dinner with family, asleep. By the time they reply, the buyer has already DMed three other agents.

This is the single biggest leak in residential real estate sales. AI receptionists were built to fix it.

## Where real estate inquiries actually come in

The channel mix has shifted hard in the last 3 years:

- **Listing portals** (Zillow, Realtor.com, Rightmove, Bayut depending on market) — generate inquiries via form
- **Instagram DMs** — younger buyers and sellers reach out via DM after seeing a listing post
- **WhatsApp** — dominant in international markets; increasingly common in US for high-touch agents
- **Direct calls** — still significant for sellers; declining for buyers
- **Open house signups** — still relevant but increasingly handled via QR codes

The problem: each channel needs a different reply, and you're physically in one place at a time. You can't be at a showing and replying to a Zillow lead in the same minute.

## What the AI handles for a real estate agent

For inbound buyer inquiries:

- **Listing-specific questions.** "Is the property at 247 Oak Street still available?" → AI checks your active listings, confirms availability, offers details.
- **Showing requests.** "Can I see the place this weekend?" → AI checks your calendar, offers 2-3 time slots that fit, books the showing.
- **Qualifying questions.** "What's your buying timeline? Are you pre-approved? What's your budget range?" — captured naturally, not interrogation-style.
- **Property feature questions.** Bedrooms, square footage, HOA fees, school district — AI answers from your listing data.
- **Price negotiation triage.** "Will they accept $X?" → AI never negotiates. It says "Let me get [agent] to discuss that with you — when's a good time to chat?"

For inbound seller inquiries:

- **Listing presentation requests.** "I'm thinking of selling — can you come look at my place?" → AI books a free 30-min CMA appointment.
- **Market questions.** "What are properties in my area going for?" → AI gives general market intel, recommends a proper CMA.
- **Existing client follow-ups.** "Any update on my offer?" → AI confirms it has the message, pings the agent immediately.

## What the AI should never do

- **Quote a property price.** Always defer to the agent for any price discussion.
- **Make commitments on terms** (closing dates, contingencies, financing).
- **Discuss neighborhood demographics** in ways that could violate fair housing laws — only objective info.
- **Make property-condition representations.** "Is the basement waterproofed?" → "I'll have [agent] confirm — that's not something I can verify."

These have legal implications. The AI handoff rules are strict.

## A real conversation flow

Here's a typical inbound buyer DM at 10pm on a Friday:

> **Buyer (via Instagram DM):** "Hi! I saw your listing for the 3-bed colonial on Maple. Is it still available?"
>
> **AI:** "Yes — 1842 Maple Street, listed at $529K, still available. Great house — open floor plan, finished basement, on a quiet cul-de-sac. Want to see it?"
>
> **Buyer:** "Yes! When can we go?"
>
> **AI:** "Sarah has Saturday at 11am or Sunday at 1pm open for showings. Which works?"
>
> **Buyer:** "Saturday 11am."
>
> **AI:** "Perfect — booked. Quick question to help Sarah prepare: are you pre-approved for a mortgage, or still in the process? And is this your primary search area or are you looking in multiple neighborhoods?"
>
> **Buyer:** "Pre-approved up to $580K. Looking in Maple Hills and Riverside specifically."
>
> **AI:** "Great — Sarah works both those areas. She'll bring a few comparable listings to show you Saturday. See you at 11am!"

Sarah wakes up Saturday morning to a fully qualified, pre-approved buyer showing up at 11am. She did nothing. The AI captured a $580K-budget buyer and qualified them at 10pm Friday night.
